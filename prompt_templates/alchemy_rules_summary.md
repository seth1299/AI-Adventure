# Alchemy Rules

Alchemy is defined as the disciplined art of transformation, specifically the craft of extracting, refining, combining, and fixing the active virtues within matter to produce a desired effect. It is not merely a collection of recipes but a systematic study of the "virtues"—the hidden powers or active qualities—of the natural and unnatural world.
The practice is governed by three primary stages: Preparation (cleaning and sorting raw materials), Refinement (turning materials into predictable reagents), and Synthesis (the final combination into a preparation). A critical fourth stage, Proving, involves testing the final product for stability and safety.

Success in alchemy relies on an understanding of the four qualities (Warm, Cool, Dry, Moist) and the seven motions (Rising, Sinking, Opening, Closing, Quickening, Stilling, Turning). Mastery requires not only technical skill in the workshop but also ethical restraint, meticulous record-keeping, and deep knowledge of botany, mineralogy, and celestial timing.

In alchemical terms, "virtue" does not refer to moral goodness but to the active quality or hidden power of a substance. The alchemist’s task is to identify these virtues, draw them forth, and arrange them so they cooperate rather than conflict.

## Classification of Alchemecial Products

Alchemical preparations are distinguished by their volume, intensity, and delivery method:

- Potions: Forceful liquid preparations for sudden, noticeable effects.
- Draughts: Milder, larger volumes that support natural processes (e.g., sleep or healing).
- Elixirs: Highly refined, concentrated, and stable; often taken in drops.
- Tinctures: Extractions of virtue into a carrier (spirit, oil, or vinegar); often used as ingredients for more complex works.
- Salves: Worked into fat or wax for topical skin application.
- Philtres: Potions affecting emotion or perception; heavily regulated due to moral implications.
- Incense: Virtues released through smoke to affect mood or the environment.

## The Four Stages of the Alchemical Process

### Preparation

The alchemist identifies, cleans, and measures raw materials. This stage is critical; flawed materials (e.g., Mooncap picked after sunrise) will lead to failed synthesis.

### Refinement

Raw materials are turned into reagents through various methods.

- Distillation: Separating volatile virtues via heat and condensation.
- Calcination: Reducing materials to ash or mineral body through controlled heat.
- Maceration: Long-term soaking to soften resins or thick roots.
- Clarification: Removing sediment through filtering or "moon-resting."

### Synthesis

The active stage where reagents are combined. A standard order of operations includes:

- Establishing the Base (the carrier liquid).
- Introducing the Primary Virtue.
- Balancing with Secondary Virtues (to soften or guide the effect).
- Adding a Binder (to ensure cooperation between ingredients). Awakening the mixture (using heat, light, or stirring).

### Proving

Testing the final product for quality. This includes examining color, scent, and stability, as well as specific tests like touching a mending draught to a cut reed to observe the reaction.

## Required Tools

A functional alchemical workshop requires specialized equipment designed to handle various virtues:

- Mortar and Pestle: Stone for minerals; ceramic for delicate flora.
- Athanor: A furnace providing steady, patient heat.
- Water/Sand Baths: Used for gentle heating to prevent scorching.
- Alembic/Retort: Distillation devices for capturing vapors.
- Vessels: Clear glass for observation; green or black glass for light-sensitive preparations.
- The Notebook: Considered the alchemist’s "second mind" for recording every proportion and reaction.

## The Seven Motions

Practical alchemy seeks to guide the "motion" of a preparation:

- Rising: Lifts, lightens, or vaporizes.
- Sinking: Grounds, stabilizes, or sedates.
- Opening: Unlocks, reveals, or softens barriers.
- Closing: Seals, protects, or finishes.
- Quickening: Speeds reactions or bodily responses.
- Stilling: Slows, calms, or suspends.
- Turning: Changes direction or transforms a quality.

## The Four Qualities

Virtues are categorized by four primary behaviors:

- Warm: Awakens, expands, stimulates, or drives outward.
- Cool: Calms, contracts, preserves, or draws inward.
- Dry: Separates, hardens, sharpens, or fixes.
- Moist: Joins, softens, carries, or dissolves.
